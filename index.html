The whole process

Selling by owner in Utah, start to finish

The steps above are the tools. This is the map: what actually happens between "let's sell it ourselves" and handing over the keys, in the order it happens.

One

Decide whether selling it yourself fits your situation

The sellers who do well on their own have two things: time to respond to calls and showings within the hour, and a timeline with some give in it. If you are relocating on a deadline or juggling a job that eats your days, that does not mean you can't do this. It means you should know it going in and plan around it.

What you save is the listing side commission. What you take on is pricing, marketing, negotiating, paperwork, and deadline tracking. Both sides of that trade are real.

Two

Price it with a real CMA, not a guess

Almost every FSBO listing that sits is priced off the wrong number: the Zestimate, the neighbor's asking price, or what the house needs to bring to make the next move work. None of those are the market. What a buyer will pay comes from recent sold homes like yours, adjusted for condition and location.

The DIY Quick CMA shows you how to pull comps and run that number yourself in about an hour. Use it to check any price anyone quotes you, including mine.

Three

Run your net sheet before you commit

The asking price is not what you keep. Between your loan payoff, title and escrow fees, prorated property taxes, any concessions, and whatever you offer a buyer's agent, the number that lands in your account is different from the number on the sign.

The net sheet does that math up front. It matters twice: once when you decide whether selling makes sense, and again when two offers arrive and the higher one nets you less.

Four

Prep the house before the photos, not after

Buyers decide fast, and their inspectors are paid to find problems. I spent years around construction before I sold homes, and the pattern holds: the money you put into the right prep comes back, and the money you put into the wrong prep does not. Deep cleaning, light, and paint pay. Major remodels on the way out the door usually do not.

The prep guide goes room by room, and the paint guide covers the colors that photograph well.

Five

Get the listing where buyers actually look

Most buyers find their home on Zillow, Redfin, or Realtor.com, and all three pull from the MLS. A yard sign and a Facebook post reach your street. A flat fee MLS listing reaches everyone shopping your price range, and it is the single highest-leverage marketing dollar an FSBO seller spends.

Photos are second. Phone photos read as "project house" even when it isn't one. The marketing guide has the full checklist.

Six

Understand the REPC before an offer arrives

In Utah, the offer that shows up is the Real Estate Purchase Contract, and once you sign it, it runs the rest of the sale: deadlines, earnest money, what happens if either side walks. Most FSBO horror stories are not about price. They are about a deadline nobody was tracking.

Read the REPC walkthrough before you list, not the night an offer comes in. Future you will be grateful.

Seven

Disclose like it protects you, because it does

Utah sellers complete a Seller Property Condition Disclosure, and the rule I give everyone is simple: when you wonder whether something is worth mentioning, mention it. A disclosed issue is a negotiation. An undisclosed one is a phone call from a lawyer after closing.

The disclosure guide walks the form section by section.

Eight

Let the title company carry you through closing

Utah closes through title companies, not attorneys, and they handle more of the finish line than most sellers expect: clearing title, holding earnest money, prorating taxes, and recording the sale. Your job is to show up with ID, sign carefully, and know that the sale is final when the deed records, not when the ink dries.

Closing day, start to finish covers what to bring and when the money lands.

The numbers

What selling by owner costs in Utah

Selling without a listing agent is not free. Plan for a flat fee MLS service, professional photos, title and escrow fees at settlement, prorated property taxes, and any repairs negotiated out of inspection. Many FSBO sellers also choose to offer buyer's agent compensation, because most Utah buyers come with an agent, and a listing that pays nothing to the person showing homes gets shown less.

Every one of those is smaller than a listing commission. Added together they are still real money, which is why the net sheet comes before the yard sign.

The honest part

Is doing it yourself right for you?

Selling on your own can save you real money. It also asks for your time, a willingness to handle paperwork and strangers in your home, and a clear head when offers and inspections get tense.

None of that is beyond you. But before you start, it's worth being honest with yourself about two things: your time and your timeline. If you have both, this guide will carry you the whole way. If one of them is tight, that's good to know now, not three weeks in.

Real questions

Questions Utah FSBO sellers ask me most

Can I sell my house myself in Utah without a real estate agent?

Yes. Utah does not require a seller to use an agent, and it does not require an attorney at closing either. You will work with a title company for the actual settlement, and you will be responsible for the contract, the disclosures, and the deadlines that an agent would normally track.

What paperwork do I need to sell a house by owner in Utah?

The main one is the Real Estate Purchase Contract, which Utah calls the REPC. You will also need the Seller Property Condition Disclosure, a lead based paint disclosure if the home was built before 1978, and whatever your title company asks for to clear title. Buyers using an agent will usually bring their own REPC to the table. The REPC walkthrough and disclosure guide cover both in plain English.

How much does it cost to sell a house by owner in Utah?

You save the listing side commission, but you rarely save all of it. Most FSBO sellers still offer something to the buyer's agent, because most buyers have one. Budget for a flat fee MLS listing, photos, title and escrow fees, prorated property taxes, and any repairs that come out of inspection. The net sheet page walks through the actual numbers.

Do I have to put my FSBO listing on the MLS?

You do not have to, but most of your buyer traffic comes from there. Zillow, Redfin, and Realtor.com all pull from the MLS. A flat fee MLS service gets you listed without hiring a full service agent. The marketing guide covers how that works.

What do I legally have to disclose when selling a home in Utah?

Anything you know about the property that a buyer would reasonably want to know, including past water intrusion, foundation work, roof age and repairs, and any known defect that is not obvious on a walkthrough. When people ask me whether something is worth disclosing, the answer is almost always yes. Disclosing costs you a conversation. Not disclosing can cost you the sale after closing.

What happens if my FSBO home does not sell?

Usually it is priced above what the comps support, or the photos are not bringing people through the door. Both are fixable without listing with an agent. If you decide you would rather hand it off, that door stays open, and you will not have wasted the work you already did.
